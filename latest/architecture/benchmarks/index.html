    Benchmarks

    What ruststream-sqlx costs on every message: throughput against a raw sqlx loop, the outbox as a plugin, and the instructions and allocations of its code.

    Against a raw sqlx loop​

    The first chart runs each scenario three ways on the stand's PostgreSQL. Raw sqlx loop is a loop written by hand that runs the statements the broker runs for the same table, in the same order, on a pool built the same way; it takes them from the dialect, so the two cannot drift apart. Crate by hand drives this crate's subscription or publisher with a loop of its own and no runtime. RustStream service is the service a user writes: a #[derive(Inbox)] table, a #[subscriber] handler and the app. The first gap is what this crate costs over the statements it runs, and the second is what the runtime adds on top.

    Each loop is a bar from its best round to its slowest, with a dot at the best round and a notch at the median. The zero of the axis is the raw loop's best round, and a dot right of zero is slower. A hollow dot is a loop whose difference from the raw loop is smaller than the spread of the rounds. The table under the chart prints the best round with the median in parentheses.

    Every message costs at least a claim, a settlement and a commit, each a round trip to the database. A row marked database-bound is one whose raw loop spent at least half of each message waiting on those round trips. The crate and the runtime then do their work inside a wait the raw loop pays as well, and the row is a lower bound on their cost rather than a measurement of it.

    The outbox as a plugin​

    The outbox works over any broker, so it is measured as a cost to an ordinary app: one app in three variants, over the stand's Redis Pub/Sub through ruststream-fred and the stand's PostgreSQL. Without the outbox is the app a user writes without one. Outbox written by hand is the same app with the record inserted before each publish, then fetched and marked in the handler, in raw sqlx. The crate's outbox is the same app with this crate's registry, its two layers and the republish. Against no outbox the chart shows what tracking a message costs at all; against the outbox by hand it shows what the crate's machinery adds to the statements a user would run anyway.

    Cost of the code​

    The cost chart counts what one message costs on the service's thread: instructions under callgrind and allocations under DHAT, in the steady state, against the stand's PostgreSQL. The count holds the runtime, this crate and sqlx's driver encoding and decoding on that thread; the database server is another process and is not counted. Cold start is what starting the service costs once: opening the pool's connections, the broker's startup checks and the first delivery. The outbox's counts run the same apps over MemoryBroker, so no transport is in their number.

    Throughput​

    A filled table drained on each database, on a pool of 16 connections, by one subscription mounted with workers(n), single deliveries or batches of 64. PostgreSQL and MySQL take the row lock form. SQLite takes the lease form, because it has no row locks. The raw loop runs n tasks on one pool, each claiming, reading and deleting until its claim comes back empty. The crate's loop runs n subscriptions on the same pool, each on a broker of its own. The service runs one subscription, which claims for all of its workers. The outbox cells mount the relay and the sink with workers(n) in each of the outbox's three variants.
